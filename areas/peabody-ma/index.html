When Water Is Spreading Through a Peabody Home

Peabody sits where three streams meet. A state planning report on the city names Proctor Brook, Goldthwaite Brook and Strongwater Brook, which flow into the North River on the way to Salem Harbor, and says they frequently flood during moderate and severe rain. One of them runs through the city center.

For a homeowner, that means a basement near any of those channels can take water in a storm that barely registers elsewhere. Call the line as soon as water starts rising, and give the dispatcher your cross street so a route can be planned.

What the March 2018 Storms Showed

The state report records that three winter storms in March 2018 brought severe flooding from rain and storm surge at high tide, plus power outages that lasted several days. The lesson for homeowners is the combination. A sump pump with no battery backup fails when the power fails, and that is how a basement that was dry at noon holds a foot of water by midnight.

After an outage, check the pit before you restart anything, and call the dispatch line if water is already on the floor. Water that crossed soil is treated as Category 3, so the crew arrives with protective gear and removal of wet porous materials follows.

Services Peabody Homeowners Call About First

Flooding leads in Peabody, so flood damage restoration and emergency water removal are the usual first calls. Once the building is pumped out, water mitigation dries it, and mold remediation follows for materials that stayed damp too long.

Peabody also has businesses with their own water-loss calls. Northshore Mall, which opened in 1958, and the retail strips along Route 1 and Route 128 are large roofs and long drain lines, so a failed roof drain or a burst sprinkler pipe floods several units at once. Tenants and landlords should settle who handles which part before demolition begins.

Peabody Landmarks and Routes

Peabody Square, Brooksby Farm and the Crystal Lake conservation area give the dispatcher fixed reference points. Crystal Lake sits in the Ipswich River watershed, and the Waters River drains the northeastern part of the city, so naming your part of town helps the dispatcher place the call.

Tell the dispatcher which side of the city you are on and the nearest cross street. The dispatcher then plans a route along Route 1, Route 128 or Route 114 and tells you what to expect next.

If Water Is Still Spreading, Do This Before the Crew Arrives

Close the valve

Shut the fixture valve or the main shutoff for the house. If the brook or storm is the source, move up and out of the lowest floor.

Power down carefully

Cut power to a wet area only from a dry spot. Never stand in water near a panel, and call 911 about any gas smell or sparks.

Lift what you can

Raise cushions, boxes and electronics onto counters and beds. Leave heavy furniture where it is if the floor is flooded.

Keep records

Photograph the waterline on the walls before it drops, and photograph the damaged belongings before anything is thrown out.
