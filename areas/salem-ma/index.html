Storm Tides and Heavy Rain in Salem

Salem is a harbor city. Salem Harbor and Beverly Harbor bound it, the Danvers River feeds into the water, the North River has an inlet in the city, the Forest River runs through the southern end and Collins Cove cuts into the shoreline. That much water edge means tides and storms are part of the water-damage picture.

The city's Collins Cove to Willows Resilience Study, published in 2024 with Salem Sound Coastwatch and funded in part by a state Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness grant, assesses both coastal flooding and rainfall flooding under current and future conditions.

Study neighborhoods

Bridge Street and East Collins Street, Webb Street and the Common, Szetela Lane, the Willows and Winter Island all appear in the city study.

Parks at the edge

Collins Cove Park and Beach, Memorial Park, Salem Willows Park and Winter Island Park sit on the shoreline the study covers.

Water supply

The Salem Beverly Water Supply Board serves Salem, with Wenham Lake, Putnamville Reservoir and Longham Reservoir among its sources.

Dispatch

When Storm Water or a Broken Pipe Hits a Salem Home

Survey respondents in the city study ranked stormwater flooding, coastal flooding and evacuation routes as top concerns. For a homeowner, the practical point is that two kinds of water show up. Water from the harbor is salt water and contaminated, while rain that cannot drain is a stormwater problem with its own contamination.

Neither is safe to touch, and both are treated as Category 3 water. Call the 24/7 line, stay above the waterline and let a crew with protective gear remove the water and the soaked materials.

Which Services Salem Callers Need First

Tide and storm losses mean flood damage restoration and then water damage cleanup to remove soaked materials and sanitize. Drying follows, and homeowners ask about water mitigation when carpet or drywall is only partly wet.

Salem has more than 20,000 housing units by the 2020 census. In any building with more than one unit, a leak upstairs reaches the units below, so the first call should include the landlord and every affected tenant.

Salem Landmarks, Streets and Routes

Salem State University, Salem Hospital, the Peabody Essex Museum, Pickering Wharf and Derby Street are the references most callers use. Routes 1A and 114 share much of the city's main road, and Route 107 enters from Lynn, and the city has no highway access of its own.

Businesses along Pickering Wharf and Derby Street often sit close to the water, so ask about flood coverage and keep a record of stock before storm season. Tell the dispatcher which cross street you are near and whether the building has a basement level.

What to Do Before the Crew Arrives If the Water Keeps Coming

Know your water

Is it harbor water, rain that cannot drain or a pipe? The dispatcher asks, and the answer shapes what the crew brings.

Stay out of floodwater

Do not wade in it. Contaminated water and live outlets are a danger to people and pets.

Protect the valuables

Lift papers, electronics and keepsakes to upper shelves or an upper floor.

Photograph the line

Mark the high-water line on walls and take photos before anything is moved.
