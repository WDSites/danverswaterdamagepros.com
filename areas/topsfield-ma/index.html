Fall Storms and Fair Season in Topsfield

Topsfield is best known for the Topsfield Fair, which has run since 1818 and fills 11 days from the last weekend of September to Columbus Day. That is also the start of fall storm season, when leaves clog gutters and heavy rain tests roofs. Fair traffic can also fill U.S. Route 1 and Route 97.

Tell the dispatcher the route you are on and the nearest cross street. Heavy fair traffic can slow any crew, so a clear address helps the plan, and no arrival time is promised.

The Ipswich River

The river runs past the Topsfield Fairgrounds and the Ipswich River Wildlife Sanctuary, and according to the watershed association, Topsfield depends on it for water.

Town water

The Topsfield Water Department, created in 1949 and part of Public Works, serves about 80 percent of the town and the Masconomet Regional School District.

Open land

Standing Water in Topsfield: First Steps and a Call

Wooded lots and river frontage mean groundwater and runoff can reach a Topsfield basement in a long rain. Essex County was designated for FEMA Individual Assistance in 2006 under declaration FEMA-1642-DR, a sign that river and rain floods reach this part of the state.

Private Wells and Floodwater

The town Water Department serves about 80 percent of Topsfield, so some homes are outside the public system and may rely on private wells. If floodwater reaches a well or well house, do not drink the water until it has been tested, and ask the local board of health or the state environmental agency how testing works.

This is separate from the restoration work in the house, which the crew handles. Mention a private well when you call so the dispatcher has the full picture.

Topsfield Roads and Reference Points

U.S. Route 1 runs southwest to northeast and is also called the Newburyport Turnpike or Old Boston Road. Route 97 runs northwest to southeast, I-95 crosses the southwestern corner and a few yards of Route 35 enter town before ending at Route 97.

Great Hill, at least 245 feet high, and Hood's Pond, which lies mostly in Ipswich, are local reference points. Give a road name and a landmark, and the dispatcher has what it needs.

What to Do While Water Is Still Spreading in Your Home

Stop the flow

Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff. If the source is outside, such as a flooded yard, keep doors shut and move up.

Mind the power

Switch off power to wet areas from a dry panel only. Report any gas smell or sparks to 911.

Move things up

Raise books, electronics and keepsakes onto high shelves, and keep pets in a dry room.

Record the damage

Photograph each room and the waterline, and save a list of damaged items.
