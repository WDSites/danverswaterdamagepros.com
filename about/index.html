About Danvers Water Damage Pros

A local dispatch line for water damage restoration and mold remediation on the North Shore.

Call (978) 252-8637

What We Do

Danvers Water Damage Pros is a dispatch service for water damage restoration and mold remediation, based at 175 Andover St in Danvers, MA 01923. When you call (978) 252-8637, a dispatcher listens to what happened, asks about the water and sends a restoration crew as soon as one is available.

The goal is a short path from a wet floor to a crew on site. The dispatcher asks where the water came from, how far it has spread and whether there is any electrical risk, then explains what to do while the crew is on the way.

Why Local Knowledge Matters

Danvers has tidal water on the Danvers River, the Ipswich River on its western border and older villages with older houses. Neighboring Peabody, Beverly, Middleton, Salem and Topsfield each have their own water sources and flood patterns, and the service pages for those towns are written around them.

Local detail helps in practical ways. It tells a dispatcher which streets are likely to take water in a storm, which town office handles permits and which questions to ask an insurer. The service areas page lists the towns covered.

What We Do Not Do

This site does not collect forms or send marketing email. The only way to reach the dispatch line is by phone. The site does not publish prices, because a price depends on what the crew finds on site. Guides on the blog explain the cost factors in plain terms.

Questions about insurance coverage belong with your insurer, and questions about health and mold belong with your physician. A crew deals with water, materials and drying.

What Happens on a First Call

The dispatcher starts with safety. If there is standing water near outlets, a smell of gas or a ceiling that is sagging, you hear about it before anything else. Next come the practical questions: where the water is coming from, whether it has been shut off and how many rooms are wet.

From there the dispatcher explains what the crew will do on arrival. The crew checks the water source, takes moisture readings and decides what can be dried in place and what has to come out. It then walks you through the scope and the price before work starts.

Standards Behind the Work

Restoration crews follow published industry practice. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference standard for professional water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 covers professional mold remediation. They describe water categories, drying classes, containment and how to confirm a structure is dry.

Those documents are why a crew talks about categories and classes when it describes your loss. A Category 1 leak from a supply line is handled differently from Category 3 floodwater or sewage, and the scope reflects that difference.

Serving Danvers and the North Shore

Danvers is the home base, and the line also covers Peabody, Beverly, Middleton, Salem and Topsfield. Those towns share the Essex County geography of tidal rivers, brooks and older housing, but each has its own water system and its own town offices. A Middleton home drinks from the same Danvers DPW Water Division system as Danvers, while Salem and Beverly rely on the Salem Beverly Water Supply Board.

That detail matters when something goes wrong in the street. Knowing which agency to call about a public main, a pump station or a flooded road saves time while the crew deals with the inside of your house.

Common Reasons People Call Us

  • A burst or leaking supply line, water heater or appliance hose.
  • A basement that took water during a storm or after a sump pump failure.
  • A ceiling stain or roof leak that has spread to drywall and insulation.
  • A musty smell or visible growth after an earlier leak.
  • A rebuild after the water is gone and the structure is dry.

Each of these has its own service page with a plain description of the work. Not every call is an emergency, and the dispatcher will tell you honestly if a visit can wait for the next day.
